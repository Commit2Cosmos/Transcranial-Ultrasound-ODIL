# The PDE term is scaled by the wave term, not by the source

The PDE residual of each (shot, frequency) is divided by `ω'²·u_ref`, the RMS size of the wave term at the band's warm start (interior only, fixed for the band), instead of the RMS of the source `q`. We chose this because an RMS of `q` is only resolution-independent for a source whose width is fixed in metres: for a source resolved at grid scale (a point source, or a Gaussian tied to `h`) the RMS of `q` over the interior scales like `1/h` while a model-error residual stays O(1), so the PDE term drifts like `h²` against the data term, i.e. halving `dx` acts like a 4x data weight. The wave-term scale does not depend on the source's shape, amplitude convention or `nt`.

## Consequences

- The scale needs a non-zero warm-start wavefield, so every band is warm-started by a Helmholtz solve; a solver-free cold start (`u = 0`) is not supported.
- The PDE residual is summed over interior and PML nodes and normalised by the interior node count, so the PML counts in proportion to its physical size and grid size does not enter the balance.
- The PML's rows are divided by `s_x s_y` (#165), so its mass coefficient is `λ_tt`, as in the interior, and its Laplacian coefficients are at most 1 in magnitude. The solution is unchanged and interior rows are untouched, so the `N_int` normalisation and the wave-term scale still hold. Without it, a thin PML (`σ_max/ω` ≈ 8 at 15 cells, 80 kHz) makes its corner rows about `(σ_max/ω)²` times the interior's, and L-BFGS with no wavefield preconditioner converges more slowly: on the default config, `rel_wavefield_error` 0.613 unweighted against 0.559 weighted after 30 outer iterations, at the same number of closures.
- Same-physical-problem scaling keeps `N_int/N` constant, so a PML-width drift shows only at a fixed resolution with a thicker PML. The balance check (`odil_wave/experiment/balance.py`) tests both axes: case `k` scales interior and PML, case `k:p` the PML alone.
- The objective is invariant to the source amplitude: `u_ref`, the PDE scale and the data scale all scale with it. So the source is not non-dimensionalised, and `source.amplitude` is exactly the peak of the waveform. The `t0²` factor on the source in `WaveEquation.sources` stays: it is the PDE's own non-dimensionalisation and keeps `u` physical.
