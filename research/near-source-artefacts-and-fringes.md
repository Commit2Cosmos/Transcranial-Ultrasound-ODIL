# Near-transducer artefacts and single-frequency fringes in ring-array FWI, WRI and ODIL

Question: how do ring-array (transmission) FWI, WRI and extended-source WRI, and ODIL handle (1) artefacts in the recovered model next to the transducers, and (2) λ/2 fringes and ringing from a single-frequency inversion? Which of their techniques carry over to this repo's joint ODIL solve, where the wavefield is an unknown? Scope: #111 (symptoms 2 and 3, H1, H3, H5), #46, #103, #75, #107; ADR-0002 and ADR-0003; [joint-block-preconditioning.md](joint-block-preconditioning.md) (**JBP**) and [model-reparametrisation.md](model-reparametrisation.md) (**MR**).

Domain terms follow `GLOSSARY.md`: **Transducer**, **Shot**, **Offset**, **Trace**, **Trace weighting**, **Interior**, **PML**, **PDE term**, **Data term**, **Regularisation term**, **Wavefield block**, **Model block**, **Model preconditioner**, **Optimised variable**, **Initial model**, **Warm start**, **Inverse crime**, **Brain region**, **Skull region**. "Source" and "receiver" mean a transducer in that role in one shot.

Every claim carries one provenance tag:

- **[S]**: established by a source. The key points to Sources. "(abstract)" means I reached only the abstract or a search excerpt. "(via VO09)" means I read the claim only in Virieux & Operto's review, not in the original.
- **[R]**: measured in this repo, in an issue or an experiment record, or read from its code.
- **[I]**: my own inference or derivation. Nothing in the literature backs it for this regime unless a tag says so.

This session ran no pilots, so there are no [P] claims. Numbers tagged [I] are closed-form arithmetic at the default config: 80 kHz, water 1500 m/s, 2 mm cells, 64 transducers on a ring of radius 0.9 × 0.125 = 0.1125 m.

## 1. Conclusion

Four results decide the options in §6.

1. **In a joint (WRI or ODIL) solve, the PDE residual is an adjoint wavefield that peaks at the receivers.** At wavefield stationarity, `r_s = −(w_d/w_p) A⁻ᴴPᵀ(Pu_s − d_s)` [I, derived in §2.1]. Operto et al. state the same thing for extended-source FWI: the scattering sources are the data residuals back-propagated from the receivers [S: Operto23 eq. 2.25–2.26]. The model gradient multiplies this by `∇'²u_s`, which peaks at the source. On a ring, every transducer is the source of one shot and a receiver in the other 63, so its cell takes the largest update [I]. This matches #111: the 64 transducer cells are 0.4% of the interior but carry 27.7% of the PDE residual at outer 1, and they all end on the 1300 m/s bound [R: #111].
2. **A diagonal model preconditioner fixes only the source side.** The model block's Gauss-Newton diagonal (`pde_diagonal`, #103) is the zero-lag autocorrelation of the virtual sources. It corrects spreading along the source-to-scatterer paths, as Shin's pseudo-Hessian does. The receiver side lives in the wavefield block [S: Operto23 §2.7; Shin01 (abstract)]. Van Leeuwen & Herrmann used this exact diagonal Newton step and **still zeroed the update on the receiver column** "to suppress artifacts in the model" [S: vLH13 §4.1; PenaltyGJI `example1.m` L63–70]. So `pde_diagonal` alone is unlikely to remove #111's transducer collapse; it needs a model mask next to the transducers [I].
3. **Every ring-array code I read fixes the model near the transducers, or only updates a region of interest.** Lucka et al. hold the sound speed outside the breast "known and fixed" [S: Lucka22 §4.2]. WISE updates only pixels inside a 128 mm circle [S: WISE15 §VI-F]. Guasch et al. mask and hold fixed a grey region in one test [S: Guasch20 Fig. 2]. Stride masks the gradient to the inner domain with a 10-cell cosine ramp by default [S: Stride `mask_field.py`]. All the ring-array codes also drop near-offset traces, keeping about a 270° arc [S: Sandhu15 §4.3; Ali24 code, ±63 of 512 elements; WISE15 §IV-C4, §VI-D]. Their stated reasons are hardware and modelling mismatch (beam pattern, point-transducer model, crosstalk), not the inverse problem itself.
4. **#111's fringes are the edge of the single-frequency wavenumber disc.** At one frequency the gradient is a truncated Fourier series over scattering wavenumbers `|K| = (2f/c₀)cos(θ/2)` [S: VO09 eq. 29–30]. A full ring covers every aperture θ, so it fills the disc `|K| ≤ 2k₀` and nothing beyond. A hard edge at `2k₀` rings with period `λ/2` = 9.4 mm, against #111's 8.8 mm [I; R: #111]. Uniform aperture also piles up sample density at the disc edge (§2.2) [I]. The one open ring-array frequency-domain code applies a "ringing removal" filter for exactly this reason: a radial low-pass on the gradient at `0.75 × 2f/c₀`, in every iteration [S: Ali24 `ringingRemovalFilt.m`, `MultiFrequencyWaveformInvkWave.m` L150, L226]. The codes also all use many frequencies: 20 frequencies at 50 kHz steps [S: Ali24 code L19], 30 kHz steps [S: Sandhu15], eight time-domain bands [S: Guasch20], and overlapping two-frequency batches [S: Aghamiry19 §3].

**No source runs a joint-space (WRI or ODIL) inversion on a ring array at skull contrast.** Every carry-over to ODIL below is my inference [I]. The joint-space sources (vLH13, vLH16, Aghamiry19, Operto23) are seismic crosswell or surface data, and one 2D ultrasound toy in vLH16. The ODIL papers have no wave-equation inverse problem [S: Karnakov24].

## 2. Mechanisms

### 2.1 Why the transducer cells collapse in a joint solve

The repo's residual is `r = λ_tt u − k∇'²u − f'` with `k = (c/c₀)²`, linear in `u` at fixed `k` [R: MR §1 result 1; `joint_odil.py` docstring]. Write it as `r_s = A u_s − f_s`. Receivers are single grid nodes (`extract_observations` samples `receiver_ij`), and sources are Gaussians with `σ_s` = 1.25 h [R: `acquisition_geometry.py`; config].

- **Wavefield stationarity** [I]. Setting `∂Φ/∂u_s = 0` for `Φ = w_p‖r_s‖² + w_d‖Pu_s − d_s‖²` gives `w_p Aᴴr_s = −w_d Pᵀ(Pu_s − d_s)`. Hence `r_s = −(w_d/w_p) A⁻ᴴPᵀ(Pu_s − d_s)`: the PDE residual is the FWI adjoint wavefield, sourced by point deltas at the receiver nodes. Operto et al. derive the same form for IR-WRI. The scattering sources are found by solving `Aᵀδb = Pᵀy` "with the weighted data residuals as source", by "obvious analogy with the so-called adjoint wavefield" [S: Operto23 eq. 2.25–2.26]. They also show that the data-assimilated wavefield "matches u* only near the receivers when m differs significantly from m*" [S: Operto23 §2.9, Fig. 7].
- **Model gradient** [I]. `∂Φ/∂k_i = −2w_p Σ_s Re(conj(∇'²u_s)_i · r_s,i)`. `|∇'²u_s|` peaks at shot `s`'s own source, which contains `f'`. `|r_s|` peaks at its receivers, near the discrete Green's function singularity of a point adjoint source. Each transducer is a source once and a receiver 63 times, so its cell is where both factors are largest. The joint L-BFGS isn't at wavefield stationarity (#96), but the wavefield block moves towards it, so I'd expect the same pattern on the path [I].
- **Why the collapse spreads** [I]. Holding the transducer cell fixed moved the collapse to its neighbours (#111 probe, `r = 0` → 186 cells at a median 1 cell away) [R]. Near the delta, the adjoint field decays only logarithmically in 2D, and the Gaussian source spreads `∇'²u` over about `σ_s`. So the hot zone is a few cells wide, not one.
- **What the data can't see** [I]. A one-cell spike has a flat wavenumber spectrum. Only about 14% of it lies inside the 80 kHz disc on this grid (§2.2), so the data term barely constrains it. The PDE term next to the transducer pays for it, and the free wavefield block can absorb most of that.
- **What a diagonal model preconditioner corrects.** In WRI the model subproblem's Gauss-Newton Hessian is diagonal, `Σ ω⁴|u_i|²` [S: vLH13 eq. 7–8]. It "corrects the descent direction ... from geometrical spreading along the incident paths connecting the sources to the scatterers (Shin et al., 2001). The missing component ... accounting for the paths connecting the scatterers to the receivers has been moved in the wavefield estimation subproblem" [S: Operto23 §2.7]. In the repo's `k`-form, the analogue is `Σ|∇'²u|²`, which is what `pde_diagonal` uses [R: #103]. It divides out the source factor and leaves the receiver factor [I].
- **The receiver footprint, in first-party code.** vLH13's crosswell example puts 51 sources on column 2 and 51 receivers on column 50 of a 51 × 51 grid [S: PenaltyGJI `example1.m` L37–38]. It updates `m` with the diagonal Newton step (L61), then repeats with `w = ones(n); w(1:51,50) = 0`, a window that zeroes the update on exactly the receiver column (L63–70). The paper says: "the region around the receivers was windowed out to suppress artifacts in the model" [S: vLH13 §4.1].

### 2.2 Why a single frequency gives λ/2 fringes

- **Wavenumber coverage** [S: VO09 eq. 29–30, after Devaney 1982, Wu & Toksöz 1987, Sirgue & Pratt 2004 (via VO09)]. For one frequency, the gradient is `Σ_ω Σ_s Σ_r Re{exp(−ik₀(ŝ+r̂)·x)Δd}`: a truncated Fourier series in `K = k₀(ŝ+r̂)`, with `|K| = (2f/c₀)cos(θ/2)` for aperture θ. "One frequency and one aperture in the data space map one wavenumber in the model space", and frequency and aperture have "redundant control of the wavenumber coverage". That redundancy is why Sirgue & Pratt can decimate frequencies. Their rule is that "the maximum wavenumber imaged by a frequency matches the minimum vertical wavenumber imaged by the next frequency" [S: VO09 p. WCC136].
- **On a full ring** [I]. θ runs from 0 (backscatter) to π (forward), so each frequency fills the whole disc `|K| ≤ 2k₀ = 2f/c₀` cycles/m. By Sirgue & Pratt's rule a single frequency is enough for coverage. Extra frequencies on a ring buy redundancy, a softer spectral edge, and protection against nonlinearity, not new wavenumbers below `2k₀`.
- **Edge ringing** [I]. A model low-passed by a sharp disc of radius `2k₀` has a 2D jinc point-spread function, whose side lobes repeat at a period close to `1/(2f/c₀) = λ/2`. At 80 kHz in water that is 9.4 mm, against #111's measured 8.8 mm [R: #111, central brain region]. Within #111's data, the 1450 m/s features and the discrete dispersion would both shorten it.
- **Edge pile-up** [I]. With apertures spread uniformly, `d|K|/dθ = −½√(4k₀² − |K|²)`, so the sample density per unit `|K|` grows like `1/√(4k₀² − |K|²)` towards the edge. The unpreconditioned gradient over-weights wavenumbers near `2k₀`, so λ/2 fringes dominate the early updates. That fits #111, where the model error is lowest around outer 5 and then rises [R: #111, #75].
- **Null space** [I]. The 2 mm grid resolves up to 250 cycles/m per axis. The 80 kHz disc has radius 107 cycles/m, which is π·107²/500² ≈ 14% of the grid's wavenumber area. JBP gives the data-count bound: `rank(S) ≤ 8064` of 15625 cells at one frequency [R: JBP §2]. Either way, most fine-scale model content is unconstrained by one band.
- **Overfitting.** In vLH16's single-frequency 2D ultrasound example, "all methods start overfitting after a few iterations. This can be countered by including more appropriate regularization or stopping the iterations early" [S: vLH16 §6.2.1]. Aghamiry et al. attribute high-frequency noise in their salt model to the "Gibbs phenomenon resulting from the frequency decimation", which their TV term removes [S: Aghamiry19 §3].

## 5. Ring-array specifics worth checking here

- **Angular sampling** [I]. Transducer spacing is `2π·0.1125/64` = 11.0 mm. That is 0.59λ at 80 kHz and 0.44λ at 60 kHz. A field arriving on the ring from anywhere on it has angular content up to about `kR` (37.7 at 80 kHz), which needs about 75 samples. So at 80 kHz the 64-element ring is just under-sampled; at ≤ 68 kHz it is not. The data say little about the medium between elements, and that is where #111's ring-adjacent cells sit. Doubling `n_transducers` is a one-key check.
- **Near-field size** [I]. `σ_s` = 2.5 mm is 0.13λ, so the source is close to a point, and the receivers are single nodes. Sampling the receivers with the same Gaussian as the sources would make the adjoint delta in §2.1 a Gaussian. That should widen and lower the receiver-side peak. It's a reciprocity-symmetric change, and #111 H1's `σ_s` sweep would test the source side.
- **Source scaling per shot.** Ali fit a complex scale per transmitter by least squares in every iteration (`scaling = (REC_SIMᴴREC)/(REC_SIMᴴREC_SIM)`) [S: Ali24 code L194–206]. That isn't needed under the inverse crime, but it will be once observations come from a separate path (#28) [I].
- **Outlier removal.** Ali zeroes the top 1% of trace magnitudes per frequency before inverting (`perc_outliers = 0.99`) [S: Ali24 code L124–133]. That's for real data.

**Suggested first experiment** [I]: a 2 × 2 design of option 1 (`r` = 0 or 4 cells) by option 2 (off or β = 0.75) at the default 80 kHz band. Each arm changes one fixed linear map on the model latent, so the factorial is clean. Read the transducer-cell `c_min`, `sat_frac`, `rmse_brain`, and the radially averaged spectrum of `c − c_true` at λ/2. Then run option 3 on the winner.

## 7. Open questions and gaps in the literature

- **No joint-space ring-array study.** vLH13's receiver window is the only joint-space near-transducer treatment I found, on a crosswell line. No source reports the ODIL equivalent.
- **Mask radius.** No source gives a radius in wavelengths or cells. vLH13 masks one column; the ROI papers mask everything outside the object. The radius here has to come from the sweep.
- **Ali24's cutoff choice.** The paper (IEEE TMI 43(8):2988) wasn't retrievable, so the reasons for 0.75 against 0.5 and for `ord` are not known. The code says only "Remove Ringing from Gradient Image".
- **Interaction with L-BFGS.** Ali's filter acts on a CG direction with an exact linearised line search; Stride's on a gradient-descent step. Neither tests it under L-BFGS with a change of variables. Lucka's insensitivity result is about diagonal, not spectral, transforms.
- **Pile-up claim.** The edge density in §2.2 is a Born, homogeneous-background, continuous-ring derivation. The skull breaks all three assumptions.

## 8. Unverified and limits

**Not retrieved in full; abstract, search excerpt or secondary only:**

- Shin et al. 2001 (abstract only, via the KIOST repository record).
- Sirgue & Pratt 2004, Devaney 1982, Wu & Toksöz 1987, Pratt 1999: only as cited and summarised in VO09 (or, for Pratt 1999, in Sandhu15). The SEG library page returned 403.
- Ali et al. 2024 (IEEE TMI): not found open access. All Ali24 claims come from the authors' public code and README.
- Bachmann & Tromp 2020: abstract only.
- Karnakov24: read through targeted questions over 100k of about 110k characters, as in JBP.

**Searched for and not found:**

- Any WRI, IR-WRI or ODIL study on a ring array or in USCT.
- A primary source that tapers the FWI gradient specifically around point sources, other than vLH13's receiver window and the ROI masks. Seismic practice mentions it widely, but I found no first-party statement I could cite.
- Stride's `filter_offsets` and `mute_first_arrival` steps: referenced by the default pipeline, absent from the open-source registry. They may be in a private build.

**Side finding, not checked against a run [I]:** in Stride's `get_grad_vp`, the velocity branch scales the preconditioner by `(2 / vp.data)**3**2` [S: `iso_acoustic/devito.py` L1090]. Python's `**` is right-associative, so that is `(2/vp)⁹`. The chain rule for a squared Jacobian would give `(2/vp³)² = 4/vp⁶`, so this looks like a precedence slip. Because `apply_prec` normalises by the sum, it would mainly reweight high-velocity regions (skull) by an extra `vp⁻³`.

**Limits:**

- §2.1 assumes wavefield stationarity; the joint L-BFGS isn't there (#96).
- §2.2 is a Born, homogeneous-background argument; it explains the fringe period, not its amplitude at skull contrast.
- No option was run, piloted or timed here.
